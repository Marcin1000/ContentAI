Transparency

AI Act and transparency

In Content AI, drafts, images and recordings are created with the help of AI models. This page explains who is responsible for what under the EU AI Act, what you see in the app and what is up to you when you publish such content. This page is not legal advice.

As of 9 October 2026

01Who is who

Content AI is an AI system for creating content, built on models from Anthropic, OpenAI and ElevenLabs. We offer it under our own name, so under the AI Act we are the provider of the AI system and responsible for marking its outputs. When you use Content AI and publish its outputs, you are the deployer of the AI system: whether published content needs a visible AI disclosure depends on you.

02What Content AI does

Content AI is a tool for writing and reviewing content. Drafts, scores and the fact check come from an Anthropic model; images, voice-over and transcription from OpenAI models; and the better voice-over from an ElevenLabs model. The model writes from the documents you add to the knowledge base and, if you turn on web search, from pages it finds online. A person always decides what gets published: you can edit the draft, revise it based on the scores and run the fact check before publishing.

03What you see in the app

For voice-over, the app says the voice is synthetic, not a human recording. We will describe other labels here once they are available in the app.

04What is up to you when you publish

Whoever publishes content prepared with AI is responsible for the publication. The AI Act (Article 50(4)) gives publishers two disclosure duties:

  • An image, recording or video that resembles real people, places, objects or events and that viewers could take as genuine: you must disclose that it was generated or altered artificially. In clearly artistic, satirical or fictional work, a disclosure that does not get in the way of the work is enough.
  • Text published to inform the public on matters of public interest: you must disclose that it was generated by AI, unless it has gone through human review or editorial control and someone holds editorial responsibility for it. A spelling check alone is not such a review.

A typical advertising text or product description is usually not “informing the public on matters of public interest”, but topics such as health, consumer safety, finance or the environment can be. If in doubt, label the text or ask a lawyer. Also tell listeners that a recording is read by a synthetic voice.

05What we do not promise

Content AI does not assess whether your publication complies with the law and does not replace legal advice. The fact check is automated and does not replace reading the text before publishing.

06Prohibited uses

Content AI must not be used to create realistic intimate images of identifiable persons without their consent or child sexual abuse material (the AI Act prohibits such uses from 2 December 2026), to impersonate others, to create fake reviews or to remove AI labels. The full list is in the Terms of Service (section 7).

07Supervision and contact

The market surveillance authority for AI systems in Poland is the Commission for the Development and Safety of Artificial Intelligence (Polish Act of 3 July 2026 on artificial intelligence systems, Journal of Laws 2026, item 1003); at EU level, the AI Office of the European Commission. Questions about content labelling in Content AI: kontakt@content-ai.net.

08Legal basis

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (the AI Act), Article 50, as amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744; Commission guidelines on the transparency obligations under Article 50 (C(2026) 5054); the Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-generated Content of 10 June 2026 (Content AI is not a signatory); the IPTC Digital Source Type vocabulary; schema.org; the C2PA specification.
